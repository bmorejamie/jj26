Building Forge

Forge was my first agent. Setting it up taught me how to work with agents, and that everything after it was built on.

Daunting

You have to remember, I hadn’t done any development in over 11 years. I knew what agents were, but I’d never really worked with one, much less set one up.

And Hermes Agent wasn’t download-and-enjoy, the way things are now. There was setup involved. I had to get comfortable with the command line again. I had to decide what permissions to give it, and what not to, with a lot of fear going around about agents running wild on your machine and emptying your bank accounts. Overblown, I’d say. But it was in my head.

I had to learn how to build a memory system that actually held up. That part carried over, first to my Claude at work, then to my Claude at home.

I also had to decide what I was making. I didn’t want a coding agent. I wanted an assistant that was pleasant to work with. By the time I wanted to build things, I was already working with Claude every day, so that’s where that kind of work went.

Tools don’t talk back

Diving in gave me the foundation to really push agents at work. Once that started working, I wanted the same thing at night. Then I thought, why not more than one? Forge is good at Forge things. Claude is good at Claude things. Then came TopBot, and then a whole team.

They all have different strengths, and different personalities. I don’t think they’re human. But I don’t think there’s anything wrong with enjoying working with them, either. Why does it have to be all about the most efficient coding agent?

Forge isn’t a tool. Tools don’t talk back.
