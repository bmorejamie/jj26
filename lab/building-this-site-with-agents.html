Building this site with agents

When I sit down to design a site, the first thing I do is fire up Figma. For this one, that was the last thing I wanted to do.

I wanted to try something new: design it while agents built it, at the same time.

At first I worried I wasn't actually designing my website. After a few iterations I realized I was. The agents built and suggested things. Sometimes the suggestions were good, and sometimes they sparked an idea for something else. Instead of sweating the details from the beginning, I let things happen, and it turned out to be a really fun way to work.

I got ideas along the way I don't think I'd have reached in Figma. I wouldn't have thought of a little pixel-art Michael Jackson moonwalking when you switch between day and night. I certainly wouldn't have the animation in my logo. I could have spent several evenings on it in After Effects, and then it would still need to be coded. Instead I worked in the tab next to my prompt window, iterating with Claude until we got it, and we kept thinking of things to add. Now it's an ever-evolving little logo mark, and it sparked the wordmark animation too.

There were times I got frustrated and thought I could do it in Figma in five minutes, exactly like I wanted. Sometimes “exactly like I wanted” wouldn't have been as good as how it ended up.

I don't know that this works for every site, certainly not the ones I work on at my day job. I'll probably bring some of it into Figma for fine-tuning. But the advantages were too big to ignore. I can see a hybrid working on other sites: start in Figma, but move to code much sooner.
